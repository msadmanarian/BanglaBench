# Research Questions & Formal Hypotheses
# Project: BanglaFactBench

The central research question guiding this investigation is:

> **Can current NLP and Large Language Model (LLM) architectures reliably verify Bengali factual claims across diverse domains, unseen sources, real-world linguistic variations, and adversarial transformations?**

To operationalize this inquiry, seven specific, empirically testable Research Questions (RQs) are formulated alongside formal hypotheses.

---

## RQ1 — Cross-Domain Generalization
*Do Bengali claim verification models generalize across distinct topical domains, or do they rely on domain-specific lexical cues?*

- **Formal Hypothesis ($H_1$)**: Models trained on in-domain claim distributions exhibit a statistically significant performance drop ($\Delta \text{Macro-F1} > 15\%$, $p < 0.05$) when evaluated on unseen domains (e.g., training on News, Politics, and Health; testing on Disaster, Finance, and Science/Technology), revealing that models capture topical vocabulary shortcuts rather than domain-invariant factual verification patterns.
- **Evaluation Setting**: Split B (Cross-Domain Split: Leave-One-Domain-Out / Domain-Disjoint Partitions).

---

## RQ2 — Cross-Source Generalization
*Do models generalize to unseen news and social media sources, or do they exploit source-specific stylistic and reporting artifacts?*

- **Formal Hypothesis ($H_2$)**: Models evaluated on claims originating from held-out publishers and platforms (Source-Disjoint Partition) show significantly lower verification performance ($\Delta \text{Macro-F1} > 10\%$, $p < 0.05$) compared to random train/test splits that share identical sources, demonstrating source leakage in standard evaluations.
- **Evaluation Setting**: Split C (Cross-Source Split: Train on Sources A, B, C; Test on Source D).

---

## RQ3 — Linguistic Robustness (Spelling, Unicode, Transliteration, Code-Mixing)
*How sensitive are Bengali claim verification systems to naturally occurring linguistic variations, including spelling errors, Unicode variations, Romanized Bengali (Banglish), and English-Bengali code-mixing?*

- **Formal Hypothesis ($H_3$)**: Standard subword tokenizers (WordPiece, BPE) and pretrained models suffer catastrophic fragmentation on Banglish transliteration and code-mixed inputs, leading to a performance degradation exceeding $25\%$ Macro-F1 relative to clean Bengali inputs, with Native script models suffering greater drops on Banglish than multilingual models like MuRIL.
- **Evaluation Setting**: Split E (Linguistic Perturbation Subsets: Typo, Unicode, Banglish, Code-Mixing).

---

## RQ4 — Adversarial Semantic Robustness
*Are state-of-the-art models vulnerable to controlled, meaning-preserving adversarial rewritings and deceptive wording manipulations?*

- **Formal Hypothesis ($H_4$)**: Adversarial paraphrasing and context-preserving word substitutions reduce claim verification F1-scores by at least $20\%$, inducing high-confidence false predictions (flip rate $> 30\%$) across both classical and transformer-based classifiers.
- **Evaluation Setting**: Split E (Adversarial Paraphrase and Wording Transformations).

---

## RQ5 — Retrieval-Augmented Verification (RAG) vs. Parametric Classification
*Does explicit retrieval of verified external evidence significantly improve veracity prediction accuracy, factual consistency, and confidence calibration compared to parametric-only classifiers and LLMs?*

- **Formal Hypothesis ($H_5$)**: Integrating a retrieval-augmented pipeline (hybrid BM25 + dense retrieval) significantly improves Macro-F1 ($p < 0.01$) over closed-book / parametric verification, reduces overconfident misclassifications, and achieves superior uncertainty calibration (lower Expected Calibration Error, $\text{ECE} < 0.10$).
- **Evaluation Setting**: Comparison across Model Families: Text-only Classifiers vs. Transformer Baselines vs. Closed-Book LLM vs. RAG Pipeline.

---

## RQ6 — Explanation Faithfulness vs. Human Plausibility
*Do model-generated rationales and explanations faithfully reflect the evidence and features responsible for veracity predictions, or do they produce convincing but unfaithful rationales?*

- **Formal Hypothesis ($H_6$)**: Explanations generated by generative models achieve high human plausibility ratings ($> 80\%$) but exhibit low quantitative faithfulness: ablated rationale inputs (Sufficiency test) fail to reproduce the original predictions in $> 35\%$ of cases, and removing key rationales (Comprehensiveness test) does not significantly diminish model confidence.
- **Evaluation Setting**: Perturbation-based Rationale Faithfulness Evaluation (Sufficiency and Comprehensiveness metrics following ERASER protocol).

---

## RQ7 — Temporal Robustness & Knowledge Drift
*Do Bengali verification systems experience severe performance degradation when evaluated on claims published after their training cutoff date?*

- **Formal Hypothesis ($H_7$)**: Evaluating models on chronologically future claims (Split D: Past $\rightarrow$ Train, Future $\rightarrow$ Test) results in progressive performance degradation ($\Delta \text{Macro-F1} > 12\%$) due to unseen entities and shifting events, with retrieval-augmented models demonstrating significantly greater temporal resilience than static parametric models.
- **Evaluation Setting**: Split D (Chronological / Temporal Split).
