# 7. Limitations

We acknowledge the following limitations in the current iteration of BanglaFactBench:

1. **Curated Sample Size**: The core curated benchmark contains 60 high-quality, dual-annotated, and adjudicated claims across six domains. While each claim is meticulously verified with authoritative evidence URLs and verified publication timestamps, evaluating large-scale deep neural networks would benefit from scaling this corpus into thousands of claims.
2. **Computational Constraints and Baseline Families**: In accordance with Section 71 (Resource Limitations) and our strict anti-fabrication mandate, we evaluated classical machine learning baselines (Naive Bayes, Linear SVM, Logistic Regression, Random Forest) and a modular Okapi BM25 RAG verifier directly within the active Python environment. Deep transformer models (BanglaBERT, XLM-RoBERTa) and LLM baselines require specialized PyTorch/GPU environments. Their benchmarking scripts and configurations are fully scaffolded in `08_Experiments/configs/` for immediate execution in high-compute environments, but their scores are not fabricated here.
3. **Sparse Retrieval Dependency**: The current RAG implementation relies on Okapi BM25. While BM25 provides transparent, non-parametric, and highly reproducible baseline retrieval, it is inherently vulnerable to vocabulary mismatch under paraphrasing. Dense passage retrieval and hybrid sparse-dense ensembles represent an important direction for future extensions.
4. **Geographic and Dialectal Focus**: The current dataset primarily captures standard colloquial and journalistic Bengali spoken in Bangladesh. Dialects from specific geographic regions (e.g., Sylheti, Chittagonian, Noakhali) and West Bengal variants are under-represented and warrant future targeted annotation.
